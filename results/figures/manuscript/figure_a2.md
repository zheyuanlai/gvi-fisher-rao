# figure_a2 caption draft

Online appendix: where the dense full-covariance cost goes as the dimension grows. Synthetic Bayesian logistic regression with $n=10d$ and a proper Gaussian prior, five problem instances per dimension, every method at a stepsize chosen by the same implementable rule as the practical benchmark. Timings are within-machine comparisons on one host with BLAS pinned to a single thread.

**(a)** Dense linear-algebra time per iteration against dimension, with the model oracle drawn separately in grey and a $d^3$ reference. The oracle dominates the algebra at every dimension measured, by a factor of 12.9 at most and 4.7 at least, and it does not stop doing so: with $n=10d$ the expectation costs $O(nQ + nd^2) = O(d^3)$ as well, so both sides of the comparison have the same order and the crossover is a property of the $n/d$ regime rather than of $d$ alone. That is worth knowing in itself: on a problem whose data grows with its dimension, the dense full-covariance algebra is not what a method pays for, which is why the deterministic wall-clock panels of the main text largely repeat the shape of their iteration-count panels. The stochastic arms, whose oracle is subsampled, are the case where the balance reverses. Within the algebra the schemes separate by their constants: at $d=200$ FR-R costs 2.1 times Sq-NGVI, the matrix exponential of the retraction against the single Cholesky update of the square-root method. At $d=10$ that spread is only 1.7.
**(b)** Objective gap after a common iteration budget, median over problem instances, with the double-precision resolution of the objective drawn as a floor; a curve on the floor converged rather than stalled. Read with (a), this is what decides whether a method's iteration count survives the cost of producing it, and here the two panels point the same way: the per-iteration costs differ by a small constant while the accuracy at a fixed budget differs by decades. At $d=200$ FR-KL still reach the resolution floor while BW-FB is left at $3.9$. The dimension enters through the admissible stepsize, not through the cost.
